Selected research and publications

Tax-and-benefit simulation model comparison

2026 · Report

Method. Compares tax-and-benefit simulation frameworks, their coverage and behavioural assumptions.

Finding. Different tools answer different fiscal questions; standard frameworks can omit wider behavioural and macroeconomic responses.

Centre for British Progress relevance. Useful for selecting and challenging a distributional-analysis framework.

Whole-system fiscal distribution analysis

2022 · Report

Method. Reviews evidence across taxes, transfers and in-kind public services.

Finding. The combined fiscal system can be more informative than the progressivity of any single instrument.

Centre for British Progress relevance. Supports integrated tax-and-spending reform analysis and policy communication.

Service-market incentives and household take-up

2025 · Journal article

Method. Randomised controlled trial of a sanitation-product marketing intervention, supplemented by interviews with sales agents.

Finding and impact. Businesses increased production and marketing, but household toilet ownership did not measurably rise; affordability and sales-agent incentives were possible constraints.

Centre for British Progress relevance. Separates provider responses from household outcomes when assessing policy delivery and distributional effects.

Research capabilities and delivery scope

Technical contribution. Stata tax-benefit microsimulation, fiscal incidence and distributional-policy analysis. International tax-and-transfer experience provides a strong foundation for the agreed fiscal question.

Engagement scoping. Agree the policy question, model interfaces, code and results review, communication outputs and delivery schedule.

Selected research and publications

Urban infrastructure counterfactual modelling

2019 · Working paper

Method. Spatial general equilibrium with multiple sectors, household groups, housing and location choice.

Finding. Coordinated transport, resilience and urban-development measures can alter income, jobs and population distribution.

Centre for British Progress relevance. Direct general equilibrium and growth-counterfactual experience.

Regional connectivity and growth modelling

2020 · Journal article

Method. Computable spatial equilibrium incorporating trade, agglomeration and labour mobility.

Finding. Estimated income gains depend on mobility and agglomeration assumptions, with uneven local effects.

Centre for British Progress relevance. Relevant to infrastructure, regional growth and model-sensitivity analysis.

Commuting investment and city-wide welfare

2016 · Working paper

Method. Spatial computable general equilibrium model in which households and firms choose locations as commuting and goods-transport costs change.

Finding and impact. Faster transport raises welfare beyond direct road users through changes in prices, rents, wages and land use.

Centre for British Progress relevance. Supports infrastructure counterfactuals, agglomeration analysis and tracing indirect policy effects.

Research capabilities and delivery scope

Technical contribution. Spatial general-equilibrium, input-output and econometric modelling, with experience leading modelling work and translating counterfactuals into public-sector advice.

Engagement scoping. Agree the policy question, model interfaces, code and results review, communication outputs and delivery schedule.

Selected research and publications

Management recruitment and United Kingdom firm productivity

2024 · Journal article

Method. Matched firm and manager data, matching and difference-in-differences with trend checks.

Finding. Recruitment is associated with productivity gains for some firms, with important differences by ownership type.

Centre for British Progress relevance. Direct relevance to United Kingdom productivity and talent policy.

Innovation protection and firm performance

2024 · Journal article

Method. Combines firm accounts and intellectual-property records in a quasi-experimental design.

Finding. Effects differ by firm size and outcome; market-position gains need not produce direct profitability gains.

Centre for British Progress relevance. Relevant to innovation, competition and causal policy analysis.

Firm clustering and regional productivity differences

2022 · Doctoral thesis research

Method. Density-based machine-learning clustering of firm locations, combined with econometric analysis of agglomeration and firm selection.

Finding and impact. Dense business clusters generate productivity externalities that differ across regions.

Centre for British Progress relevance. Relevant to place-based growth, productivity and separating local externalities from firm-composition effects.

Research capabilities and delivery scope

Technical contribution. Python, pandas and personal GitHub work; data preparation, predictive modelling and official-statistics validation. Tax-modelling background is confirmed by Phareyes; model-specific scope can be agreed during briefing.

Engagement scoping. Agree the policy question, model interfaces, code and results review, communication outputs and delivery schedule.

Selected research and publications

Electricity pricing and distributional incentives

2020 · Working paper

Method. Cross-country tariff-design comparison covering cost recovery, equity and technology incentives.

Finding. Common pricing structures can weaken affordability targeting, investment incentives and cost recovery.

Centre for British Progress relevance. Transferable subsidy and distributional-policy analysis.

Institutional patterns in infrastructure reform

2017 · Working paper

Method. Multi-country, long-run descriptive dataset on reform timing and scope.

Finding. Reforms are often selective, stalled or reversed; the analysis describes adoption rather than causal impact.

Centre for British Progress relevance. Relevant to implementation constraints and policy feasibility.

Public debt risks and private financing conditions

2021 · Doctoral research summary

Method. Compares sovereign borrowing yields with economic growth and examines how perceived public-debt sustainability can affect corporate borrowing through investor beliefs.

Finding and impact. Low headline borrowing rates can mask shrinking room for fiscal deficits; yield-growth gaps can expose risks not captured by median debt ratios alone.

Centre for British Progress relevance. Connects fiscal sustainability, investment incentives and private-sector financing conditions.

Research capabilities and delivery scope

Technical contribution. Financial and tariff modelling, subsidy analysis and empirical infrastructure research. Relevant to a defined regulatory or distributional-policy contribution.

Engagement scoping. Agree the policy question, model interfaces, code and results review, communication outputs and delivery schedule.

Selected research and publications

Sequential policy allocation under uncertainty

2024 · Journal article

Method. Sequential treatment-allocation algorithms, performance bounds and R-based numerical experiments calibrated to employment-programme data.

Finding. Computational demands and treatment-effect heterogeneity can limit welfare gains.

Centre for British Progress relevance. Strong evidence of algorithm implementation, sensitivity analysis and policy evaluation.

Uncertainty-aware treatment-policy learning

2022 · Preprint, revised 2023

Method. Empirical welfare updating using a variational approximation, experimental data and simulations.

Finding. Derives convergence results and probabilistic treatment rules that reflect uncertainty.

Centre for British Progress relevance. Relevant to evidence-led allocation and uncertainty.

Policy allocation under estimation uncertainty

2022 · Working paper, revised 2023

Method. Places a prior over treatment-allocation rules, updates it using empirical welfare and develops a computational approximation; illustrated with experimental employment-programme data.

Finding and impact. Derives welfare guarantees for probabilistic treatment assignment while allowing for uncertainty about which policy works best.

Centre for British Progress relevance. Supports policy targeting, uncertainty analysis and assessment of welfare-oriented decision rules.

Research capabilities and delivery scope

Technical contribution. R-based numerical experiments, statistical programming and causal inference. Policy-learning research supports model sensitivity, assumptions and welfare-performance analysis.

Engagement scoping. Agree the policy question, model interfaces, code and results review, communication outputs and delivery schedule.

Selected research and publications

Cash transfers and household employment decisions

2021 · Journal article

Method. Randomised programme evaluation examining household bargaining and parental labour-supply responses.

Finding. Employment responses differ with household bargaining conditions; an average effect can hide meaningful heterogeneity.

Centre for British Progress relevance. Direct welfare and labour-supply expertise.

Long-run employment-training evaluation

2024 · Journal article

Method. Experimental and administrative data with intention-to-treat and treatment-effect estimates.

Finding. Average effects on many labour outcomes are limited, with gains concentrated in some participant and provider groups.

Centre for British Progress relevance. Relevant to skills, participation and rigorous impact evaluation.

Evidence on digital training and employment transitions

2025 · Working paper and evidence review

Method. Reviews 26 impact evaluations of digital-skills training, online labour-market programmes and newer training delivery methods.

Finding and impact. Some interventions improve access to technology-related jobs or earnings, but overall employment effects are inconsistent and long-term evidence remains limited.

Centre for British Progress relevance. Supports skills-policy design, evidence appraisal and judging whether targeted gains translate into wider employment benefits.

Research capabilities and delivery scope

Technical contribution. Randomised evaluations, administrative-data analysis and labour-supply research. Relevant to designing and evaluating employment, welfare and skills interventions.

Engagement scoping. Agree the policy question, model interfaces, code and results review, communication outputs and delivery schedule.

The job description

Assessment against Centre for British Progress’s official Senior Economist brief.

Build a general-equilibrium model of tax and government-spending changes, including detailed distributional analysis. Develop growth-supporting reforms, explain the effects to policymakers and lead tax, spending and local-government-finance workstreams.

  • Generating and reviewing artificial intelligence code, including reward hacking
